Hamlet · visual production research · proposed direction

A town made as a small,
cared-for object.

An orthographic architectural diorama: editable geometry, quiet daylight, roads with an actual modular rhythm, and destinations recognisable from shape before a label is read.

Composition study

Direction: architecture first, then scale witnesses. Buildings receive a single identifying silhouette, an entrance cue and only enough material detail to catch the soft light.

Six distinct destinations

The roof line, entrance and one adjacent prop need to name the place before a label does.

Home

Two-volume gable, porch, planted threshold.

Post

Mail flag, slot, parcel bench, small van.

Library

Stepped book roof, deep entry, return slot.

Studio

Asymmetric shed, tall work glazing, board outside.

Café

Low corner block, broad awning, two tiny tables.

Greenhouse

Thin pitched frame, visible planting tables.

Modelling and light rules

  1. Snap the town to a 1 m logical grid. A lane is 2 units; homes are 3 × 3 or 3 × 4; civic buildings are 5–7 units across.
  2. Build in layers. Base, route, curb, shell, roof, threshold, prop kit. Keep them editable and separately stateful.
  3. Carry detail on roofs and thresholds. Add fascia thickness, shallow overhangs, a few large seams, and one interruption: skylight, chimney, gutter or planter.
  4. Use orthographic camera. Start at 45° yaw / 35.2644° downward elevation. Orthographic scale frames the diorama; do not chase the crop by distorting buildings.
  5. Light like a miniature outside. Cool fill plus one soft Sun key. Short contact AO for feet, eaves, curbs and pots only.
  6. Keep materials matte. Plaster, timber, road and foliage need high roughness. Glass needs tint, thickness and a dark frame.

What would make it feel cheap

  • Every building gets the same roof pitch, chimney and large front label.
  • Strong bloom, pure white walls, black outlines or lots of saturation are used to force readability.
  • Roads become uninterrupted grey bands instead of routes with planted corners, crossings, benches and setbacks.
  • AO becomes wide dirty shading rather than close contact separation.
  • Every edge is rounded equally, making architecture look like generic toys.
  • People, cars and trees are as visually loud as destinations.
  • Shadows, greenery or signs are baked into one texture, eliminating future interaction states.
