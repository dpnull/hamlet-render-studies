Camera makes a town, not a catalogue.

Use an orthographic three-quarter overview for comparable room bays and parallel street edges. Hold a generous ground border around the subject.

Framing guardrail

Put taller masses in the upper/back third. A roof, sign or tree never hides an entrance, stair landing or the room’s main action.

Open front, not transparent shell.

Keep rear partitions shallow. The missing front is an editable spatial condition with a frame, a threshold and shadow, not an alpha channel pasted over furniture.

Let circulation carry the story.

A 2m shared street becomes useful when it meets a landing, bench, planter or delivery pause. Keep the clear walking line intact: movement is the connective tissue.

Terrace rhythm

Street level → half-level planted porch → one-storey room step. Use three legible levels before adding more height.

Construction grammar

These are the reusable constraints for a spatial renderer. They should make a new block feel like a cousin of the first, while still letting its use change.

  1. Snap slabs, openings, planting beds and stair landings to a 1m logical grid.
  2. Compose each bay from slab, side/rear wall, cap, frame, threshold, route edge and prop group.
  3. Expose one room, then give the next bay a closed or screened facade to restore quiet.
  4. Furniture begins on the rear or side wall. Keep a clear entrance-to-action path.
  5. Use roof caps, stairs, curbs and frames for light-catching bevels; keep broad walls matte.
  6. Give each room a single verb, three major objects and one obvious threshold cue.

Scale witnesses: 1.65m adults, 2.1m door heads, 0.75m table tops, 4.2m small utility vehicle. At 390px, fix geometry before increasing saturation or labels.

Light rule

Soft directional key plus cool fill. Put contact shadow under roof caps, stairs, trees and wheels only; do not paint a global dirt mask.

Reject it if

Every room needs a word label; stairs terminate without a landing; foreground objects obscure entrances; all bays expose their contents; or a prop has no job in the person–route–room story.
