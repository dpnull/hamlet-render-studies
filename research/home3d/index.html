Hamlet · 3D home research · proposed visual direction

One small room.
A place for every kind of day.

A polished cutaway made as a tiny object: sun through deep timber, a warm workbench, and enough empty floor for the bean to decide where to go. The room uses objects as invitations, never as a menu.

Single-room composition

Open-front and open-right gives the viewer a clean read of the room. The daylight wedge crosses the desk and fades before the sofa, so the object hierarchy arrives through light rather than labels.

Material hierarchy

Walls stay quiet. Tactile material gathers where a hand or the bean touches something.

Five objects with a real job

Each owns a function, a readable silhouette and a small, physical interaction. Their locations form a loop rather than a row of feature stations.

Mail tray

Entry console, red envelope, three hooks. Bean stops and lifts the day’s letter.

Pinboard

Linen panel and real paper depth. One note rises a little when selected.

Lesson desk

Window-facing folio, small lamp and footstool. The bean can settle here without excess clutter.

Idea bench

Peg rail, sketchbook, stamp and drawers. A drafted card folds or lands on the bench.

Sofa rest

Low cushions and a bean-height ottoman. Rest reads as care, with no score effect.

Bean route and clearance

Block movement before the detail pass. The bean needs a continuous 1.05-unit loop and 0.85 clear at an interaction point.

Build it like a miniature

  1. Start with the 7.8 × 6.2 unit plinth, two walls and deep 3-pane window. Check the composition in clay.
  2. Place the furniture around the loop; confirm every function is readable without text.
  3. Use crisp walls and only 0.10–0.14 unit chamfers where a hand would touch.
  4. Key from the window with a broad 5100 K source; use cool 15–20% fill and close contact shadow only.
  5. Spend the detail budget on paper edge, wood joinery, pins, tray lip, lamp and cushion seam. Remove generic clutter.
